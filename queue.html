        About auto-advance. It works two different ways, because the two sources differ. YouTube publishes a player API, so this page is genuinely told when a video ends and advances on that — exact, and it respects a pause. Suno's embed is a plain cross-origin frame that reports nothing, and their audio stream is encrypted, so the track cannot be played here and watched for its end either. For Suno rows auto is therefore a timer over the track's known length, with the countdown kept on screen: right when you listen straight through, and wrong if you pause inside the frame, which is the one thing it cannot see. It starts when you press play rather than when the track loads — the page cannot read inside the frame, but it can see the window hand focus to it, which is the moment somebody pressed something. Only that first press is visible: once focus is in the frame, further clicks there raise no event at all — measured, not assumed — so pausing cannot be detected and the countdown keeps running while Suno's does not. That is the one case it gets wrong, which is why it stays on screen rather than working silently. Click it to start a clean run from the top.

        Dropping into the chat. A link dropped anywhere on the chat is posted as a message, which is how a guest suggests something. If you own the queue it is queued as well — suggesting a track to yourself and then pressing your own button is a step that does not need to exist. Anything without a link in it is just posted.

        Album playlists. A YouTube Music album link (its id starts OLAK5uy_) will not play here. The embedded player refuses to unpack one — it loads a single track and then shows its own error without telling this page anything, which is why such a row is marked in the list and offers a link out. Ordinary playlists are fine. Individual tracks from the album are fine too, if you would rather queue them one at a time.

        YouTube Premium and ads. Ads in the embed are a cookie problem rather than a Premium problem. The player is a third-party frame, and a browser blocking third-party cookies — most now do by default — leaves YouTube unable to see that you are signed in, so it serves ads to what looks like a stranger and never consults your account. The yt sign-in button asks your browser, on your click, to let the YouTube player have its own cookies here; you get a prompt and you decide. Granting it and letting the player reload is what makes Premium apply. This page never sees a cookie or an identity either way, and if your browser refuses, the only remaining fix is to open the track on YouTube itself.
