    What is public here. A queue that is listed appears on this page to anyone, along with its name, its author's display name, and when it was made. Display names are self-chosen and unverified. An owner can set a queue to unlisted on its own page, which keeps it off this list — that means unadvertised, not private, since anyone holding the link can still open it.

    Live, and who is counted. A queue reads live while its owner has it open in a browser somewhere, and the number beside it is how many people have it open — one person with three tabs is one person, and nobody is named. Having a queue open is what publishes this; the database holds one row per open tab and deletes it itself the moment that tab closes or the connection drops, so leaving needs no button and there is nothing to forget to switch off. A backgrounded tab is still a tab, so live means open, not necessarily listening. Owner away means somebody is in there without them: a shared queue only advances while its owner's player is running, so it is waiting rather than playing.

    Who can delete. Only the person who made a queue, enforced by the database rather than by hiding the button. Identity is anonymous and lives in your browser: clear its site data and you become someone else, and your queues would stay here with nobody able to remove them.
